\begin{abstract}
An organisation already publishes a prediction service: fixed probability vectors that downstream
recipients consume and cannot be asked to re-accept. We ask what happens when it adds a
\emph{second}, reusable feature channel for one recipient without changing a single published
number, and tries to limit what that recipient---and a coalition of recipients---can \emph{newly}
infer about sex and race. We contribute an evaluated formulation of that problem and two completed
studies of one mechanism family for it. The mechanism keeps the teacher information the published
outputs do not explain and chooses directions by maximising linear reconstruction of that residual
minus conditional-moment penalties, conditioning on the released service probabilities through
cross-fitted nuisances. Penalties can be local to one recipient or specific to a coalition's joint
view. Five findings. (i) Exact service preservation is structural and holds bitwise on all 210
released views; it does not imply stable service \emph{accuracy}, which moves by up to $0.015$ nats
across one year. (ii) On a locked, previously unused survey year the coalition-conditioned penalty
beats both an equal-strength and an equal-total-mass local control under simultaneous 95\%
intervals: 14 of its 16 sensitive endpoint cells are strictly better, none is worse, and two are
unresolved. The registered rule---at least one strictly better and none strictly worse, per
weighting---passed in all four cells. (iii) That rule pairs with a \emph{point} constraint on
residence utility, and the interval evidence does not establish non-inferiority at the same
$0.001$ margin: zero of four comparisons, under any procedure we computed. (iv) Under identical
attacks the mechanism is significantly worse than a frozen adversarially trained channel of the
same width on all four sensitive endpoints under both weightings, and that ordering survives
matching the two on average utility in both directions. (v) A refinement---a penalty nonlinear in
the channel, plus a rank-8 compression---moves measured recovery substantially in the intended
direction on development data, but all eight registered coordination cells still fail, the best
candidate remains significantly worse than the neural channel on local sex, and an exploratory
cross-year look widens rather than narrows that gap. The refinement also turned out to be
coordinate-dependent: a search that only rotates the released subspace, changing nothing an
attacker can recover, reaches a large share of its training-objective gain. We report an
evaluation contribution and two negative method results, and we list every claim of our own that
this revision withdraws or narrows.
\end{abstract}

\section{What this paper is, and what it is not}
\label{sec:contribution}

A service that already publishes predictions can hand one recipient extra features built only from
what those predictions leave unexplained, and can steer those features away from directions that
would let that recipient---or that recipient plus a colluding one---guess sex or race better than
the published predictions already allow. We show the steering knob works on a sealed new year of
data against its own local controls; that a refinement of it moves recovery further in the intended
direction on development data; and that neither version is competitive with an adversarially
trained channel of the same size.

\textbf{This is an empirical and problem-formulation paper, not a method paper.} The useful output
is the formulation---fixed published outputs as a hard constraint, recipient-specific and
coalition-specific audit roles---together with the evaluation design and two honest negative
results. It is not a deployable mechanism and we do not present it as one. No claim here
establishes universal privacy, conditional independence, an upper bound on mutual information, or
the failure of any mathematical method family.

Two completed studies are reported. \textbf{Study~1} is a locked temporal transport of a frozen
14-interface slate to a previously unused survey year (\S\ref{sec:results1}). \textbf{Study~2} is a
development study of a nonlinear-moment refinement and a rank intervention
(\S\ref{sec:results2}). The main text keeps them apart by what each is evidence \emph{for}, not by
the order in which they were run. \S\ref{sec:withdrawn} lists every claim this revision withdraws
or narrows, with the evidence for each.

\paragraph{One paragraph for a colleague.} We study a release setting that existing invariant- and
fair-representation work does not evaluate: an organisation has already published prediction
outputs it cannot revise, and wants to add a reusable feature channel for one recipient while
limiting what that recipient, and that recipient colluding with another, can newly infer about
protected attributes. Within that setting we show that a penalty built specifically from the
coalition's joint view measurably reduces recovery relative to local-only penalties on a sealed
evaluation year---but that the whole mechanism family we tried remains clearly behind an
adversarially trained channel of the same width, so what we are offering is the evaluated problem
and the measurement design, not a competitive algorithm.

\section{Threat model and interface, in plain language}
\label{sec:interface}

Each person has covariates $X$, sensitive attributes $S$ (sex, 2 classes; race, 9 classes) and task
labels. Before anything in this paper happens, two services are already frozen and published:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item $H_A(X)\in[0,1]^4$ --- the full income-over-\$50k and employment probability vectors,
  released to recipient~A;
\item $H_B(X)\in[0,1]^2$ --- the full public-coverage probability vector, released to recipient~B.
\end{itemize}

A mechanism may add a channel $Z\in\mathbb{R}^{16}$ \emph{for A only}. Recipient B's release is
unchanged. So the three views are: A sees $(H_A,Z)$; B sees $H_B$; the coalition AB sees
$(H_A,Z,H_B)$. Throughout, $H$ with no subscript means the no-channel interface: what an attacker
can already do from the published service alone.

Three names recur and are fixed here once.

\begin{description}\itemsep2pt
\item[\normalfont\textbf{$J$, the frozen neural channel.}] A 16-dimensional adversarially trained
channel, recovered from the earlier line of work and never retrained here. It is the strongest
comparator in the study. It is \emph{internal}: it is not an external published baseline, and no
result below should be read as a comparison against a state-of-the-art system
(\S\ref{sec:related}).
\item[\normalfont\textbf{Local controls, $L1$ and $L2$.}] Spectral arms whose penalty uses only
recipient A's own view. $L1$ is matched to the candidate on penalty \emph{strength}; $L2$ is
matched on total penalty \emph{mass}. They exist so that a coalition effect cannot be confused with
``more penalty''.
\item[\normalfont\textbf{Coalition condition, $C1$.}] The same construction with an additional
penalty term built from the coalition's joint view $(H_A,Z,H_B)$. It is the candidate.
\end{description}

The adversary holds labelled data from the release year, fits fixed attack families on its own
pool, selects on a validation pool, and sees only its legal view. The policy authorises A for
income, employment and residence, B for coverage and commute, and forbids sex and race everywhere,
coverage and commute for A, and income, employment and residence for B---eleven forbidden roles.
Every published function, including the preprocessing, the released map and the neural arms' public
auxiliary heads, is in scope, and coalition attacks may use any legal projection of A's and B's
candidates.

\textbf{The A-only limitation is real and bounds every claim below.} Only one recipient receives a
channel. Nothing here demonstrates joint optimisation of channels for several recipients, and the
coalition penalty is a constraint on one channel given another recipient's fixed view, not a
multi-channel design.

\section{Why the published outputs are retained}
\label{sec:retained}

The constraint that makes this problem different from representation learning is that $H_A$ and
$H_B$ have already been published. Downstream consumers have built on those exact numbers; a
revision is a migration, not a deployment. So the mechanism may append but not modify: $Z$ is
concatenated to $H_A$ and never mixed into it. Exact preservation is then \emph{structural} rather
than measured---there is no perturbation whose size must be bounded---and we verify it bitwise on
every released view rather than certifying it statistically. This also means preservation is
\emph{easy}: appending a vector while leaving another untouched is a trivial observation about
concatenation, not a theorem, and we claim nothing more for it.

Two things it does not give us. It does not mean the service stays \emph{accurate}: on identical
released vectors, employment log loss rises by $0.0138$ nats and income falls by $0.0145$ between
the two years (Table~\ref{tab:criteria}). And it does not constrain what the appended channel
discloses, which is the entire subject of this paper.

\section{The mechanism}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The original fixed-matrix construction}

Let $T$ be a frozen PCA32 teacher. Build $\phi(T)=[\,\text{standardised }T,\ 96 \text{ random
Fourier features}\,]$ with a median-heuristic bandwidth \citep{rahimi2007random}. Let $q_A$ be the
intercept and all degree-2 monomials in A's two positive-class service probabilities; $q_{AB}$ adds
B's. Residualise by SVD least squares, $V_0=\phi-q_A B_\phi$ and $R=T-q_A B_T$, then eigen-whiten
$V_0$ to $V$ with $V^\top V/n=I$. Utility is $U=C_{VR}C_{RV}$: for $W^\top W=I$,
$\operatorname{tr}(W^\top UW)$ is exactly the training reduction in least-squares reconstruction
error of the residualised teacher. Protection matrices use household-grouped 3-fold cross-fitted
multinomial-logistic nuisances $m_j$ (cross-fitting in the sense of \citealp{chernozhukov2016dml};
we claim none of that work's orthogonality or rate guarantees for our moments) and, for each
retained basis column $b_k$,
\[
G_{jk}=\frac{1}{n_j}\sum_i V_i\, b_k(H_i)\,\bigl(\mathrm{onehot}(S_j)_i-m_j(H_i)\bigr)^{\!\top},
\qquad P_j=\textstyle\sum_k G_{jk}G_{jk}^{\top}\ \text{(trace-normalised)},
\]
averaged over local roles (basis $q_A$: sex, race, coverage), coalition roles (basis $q_{AB}$: sex,
race) and a marginal variant (constant basis). The released map is the top-16 eigenvectors of
$U_{\mathrm{norm}}-\lambda P$, giving arms S0 ($\lambda=0$), M025/M1 (marginal), L025/L1/L2 (local)
and C025/C1 (local $+$ coalition). Inference needs only $T$ and $H_A$; $H_B$ enters fitting and
auditing only.

\subsection{The refinement studied in Study~2}
\label{sec:refinement}

Study~2 replaces the fixed penalty matrix with a penalty computed from degree-two monomials of the
channel output itself, so that the objective is no longer of the form
$\operatorname{tr}(W^\top AW)$ for any $W$-independent $A$. It also adds a predeclared rank-8
variant. Both are development-only interventions. The refinement was fitted, audited and then found
to be \emph{coordinate-dependent}; \S\ref{sec:rotation} reports that diagnostic. \textbf{The defect
was not repaired in the maps that were evaluated}, so every Study~2 number below is a number for
the defective objective, and should be read that way.

\subsection{What is a theorem here, and what is not}
\label{sec:scope}

The eigenvector step is Ky Fan's maximum principle for a \emph{fixed} empirical matrix
\citep{fan1949weyl}. It certifies nothing about the choice of features, basis, nuisances, rank or
attacker, and with $r$ fixed at 16 it can retain directions whose penalty exceeds their utility.
Crucially, it is a statement about the \emph{original} family only: the refinement of
\S\ref{sec:refinement} has no fixed matrix to take a spectrum of, so it inherits no optimality
statement whatever. Selecting rank by eigenvalue sign is not our idea and is not new: it is SARL's
Theorem 3, OptNet-ARL's Theorem 4.1 and K-TOpt's Corollary 4.1
\citep{sadeghi2019sarl,sadeghi2021optnetarl,sadeghi2022ktopt}. We fixed $r=16$ to match the width
of the neural baselines, which is a comparison choice, not an optimum.

The moment condition is a finite subset of the $L^2$ characterisation of conditional independence
that kernel CI tests use \citep{zhang2011kci,strobl2019rcit}. Precisely, it is Lemma 2(v) of
\citet{zhang2011kci}, attributed there to Daudin: our centring on the $S$ side is algebraically
equivalent to residualising the $(Z,H)$ side, since with $\tilde f=f-\mathbb{E}[f\mid H]$ we have
$\mathbb{E}[f(Z,H)(\mathbf 1\{S=c\}-m_c(H))]=\mathbb{E}[\tilde f\cdot\mathbf 1\{S=c\}]$. The
characterisation quantifies over \emph{every} square-integrable $f$; we test
$f\in\{Z_\ell b_k(H)\}$---linear in $Z$, degree-2 in $H$, with estimated nuisances and finite
samples. Consequently vanishing fitted moments do not establish conditional independence, and a
non-zero moment may reflect nuisance error rather than leakage. Two fixtures make this concrete and
are carried in the code. An XOR channel has exactly zero marginal moment while $S=ZH$ recovers it
perfectly; it is detected by an interaction term \emph{in $H$}, so enriching the conditioning side
suffices. A magnitude channel---$(Z,S)$ uniform on $\{(-1,0),(1,0),(-2,1),(2,1)\}$ with $H$
constant---has exactly zero first moment, yet $\mathbf 1\{|Z|>1.5\}$ recovers $S$ exactly; because
$H$ is constant, \emph{no} enrichment of the conditioning side can ever see it, and only a
nonlinear function of $Z$ can ($\mathbb{E}[Z^2(S-\tfrac12)]=0.75$). The attacks that defeat our
arms on ACS are MLPs and boosted trees, i.e.\ the second kind.

With population-optimal log-loss predictors, the $H$ versus $(H,Z)$ risk difference equals
$I(S;Z\mid H)$. Our validation-selected finite attacks do not attain those infima, so the reported
differences bound it in neither direction---indeed some are negative---and mutual-information
estimation has its own formal limits \citep{mcallester2020formal}. We report no certified
information quantity. For the withholding control, an independent visible branch
$B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ gives $I(S;B,Z_B\mid H)=p\,I(S;Z\mid H)$ and expected routed loss
$(1-p)L_H+p\,L_{HZ}$; the branch must be persistent, since independent re-draws would eventually
reveal $Z$.

\paragraph{Invariance, stated carefully.} For an invertible coordinate change $W\mapsto WQ$ with
$Q$ orthogonal, the released \emph{information} and the unrestricted Bayes-optimal prediction risks
are unchanged, because the two releases are measurable functions of one another. That is a
statement about information and about optima over all measurable predictors. It is \emph{not} a
statement that every finite trained attacker, or every feature-restricted penalty, behaves
identically on the two releases: a fitted MLP at a fixed budget is a particular algorithm applied
to particular coordinates. We use the first statement only, and only where it is what the argument
needs.

\section{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:related}

The closed-form line of Sadeghi, Dehdashtian and Boddeti already solves this shape of problem. SARL
\citep{sadeghi2019sarl} takes eigenvectors of a utility-minus-$\lambda$-dependence matrix;
OptNet-ARL \citep{sadeghi2021optnetarl} keeps closed-form ridge players inside a learned encoder
and already handles several sensitive attributes through several $\lambda$; K-TOpt
\citep{sadeghi2022ktopt} adds random Fourier features and evaluates on Folktables
\citep{ding2021retiring}; U-FaTE \citep{dehdashtian2024ufate} adds a \emph{conditional} penalty.
Our marginal arms are, up to parametrisation, SARL's construction on whitened random-feature
residuals. \textbf{Reusing a known solver, a conditional-moment characterisation or a rank rule is
attribution, not innovation}, and we list the reused components explicitly in
\texttt{RELATED\_WORK\_SCOPE.md}.

Three things differ, and we state their weight honestly. \emph{What we condition on:} U-FaTE's
conditional measure is stratification over a discrete label $Y$, and its own text scopes the
equivalence to ``when $Y$ is not a continuous label''; $H_A$ is a pair of continuous probability
vectors, so stratification is unavailable and we substitute cross-fitted nuisance residuals. $H$ is
also a \emph{released artifact} rather than the label to be predicted---a different object even
setting continuity aside. \emph{What utility means:} reconstruction of a residualised teacher
rather than dependence with a label. \emph{Recipient structure:} separate local and coalition
penalties, with only one recipient receiving a channel.

The first two are adaptations of known components, not new optimisation principles. The third we
did not find in the surveyed literature, and the nearest candidate is weaker prior art than its
title suggests: the multi-consumer information-theoretic line \citep{sankar2013utility} designs
\emph{one} sanitised release consumed by many users who differ only in private side information,
with no per-recipient release, no collusion term, and no constraint to preserve an already-published
output. Linear concept erasure \citep{belrose2023leace,holstege2025splince} addresses a different
guarantee (exact \emph{linear} erasure) on a representation the releaser owns and may edit; LEACE
scopes its guarantee to linear adversaries explicitly. Adversarial removal is known to leave
recoverable information \citep{elazar2018adversarial}, and least-privilege learning has fundamental
limits \citep{stadler2024leastprivilege}.

Two further neighbourhoods are worth separating from ours. Conditional fair representation learning
conditions on a \emph{label} for separation-style criteria \citep{zhao2019conditional,hwa2024enforcing},
not on a released prediction, and again designs the representation from scratch.
Information-theoretic privacy gives the quantities our log-loss endpoints relate to---leakage and
utility both become mutual information under log loss \citep{makhdoumi2014funnel}, with
perfect-privacy conditions \citep{rassouli2021perfect} and robustness of leakage measures to side
information \citep{liao2019sideinfo}---but assumes a known joint distribution and a randomised
mapping, whereas our released map is deterministic and our attacks are fitted. Task-agnostic
private representation learning \citep{arevalo2024tappfl} targets attribute inference without a
known downstream task, again with no fixed prior output and one recipient.

\paragraph{The scope of ``we did not find it''.} A bounded search cannot establish novelty and we
make no priority claim. We distinguish four questions and answer them separately in
\texttt{RELATED\_WORK\_SCOPE.md}: whether a prior formulation \emph{can express} this setting
(several can); whether it supplies an \emph{algorithm} applicable here (several do, with
adaptation); whether it \emph{evaluated} recipient-specific and combined-access policies with
unchanged existing outputs (we did not find one that did); and whether our study demonstrates an
effect beyond its own controls (\S\ref{sec:coalition}: yes, against $L1$ and $L2$ on one sealed
year). ``We did not find a directly matching evaluation'' is the claim; ``no prior method can do
this'' is not, and would be false for at least the adaptation question.

\paragraph{No external baseline was run, and what that costs us.} SARL, K-TOpt, U-FaTE, OptNet-ARL,
LEACE and SPLINCE are discussed but never executed here. So this paper cannot claim competitiveness
with any of them and does not. In particular, $J$ is an \emph{internal} adversarially trained
channel, not an external benchmark, and where $J$ beats our arms that is evidence about $J$, not
about the state of the art. \texttt{RELATED\_WORK\_SCOPE.md} specifies what a fair external
comparison would require, including two constraints that are easy to get wrong: if $H$ must remain
immutable then LEACE/SPLINCE may act only on the auxiliary channel $Z$, and any guarantee they
carry then has that scope; and a baseline may not consume residence labels if it is to be compared
on transfer to a residence task excluded from representation fitting. Any privileged-label variant
is an oracle and belongs outside the main comparison.

and validation, and evaluation.

\paragraph{Study~1: locked transport.} Fourteen interfaces per seed---H, E, A0, L025, L20, J
(frozen adversarially trained channels, recovered without retraining) and the eight spectral
arms---i.e.\ 42 interface instances, of which 24 are the new maps, frozen before any reserved-label
evaluation. Development was ACS 2018. Transport was ACS 2017, admitted on provenance and schema
alone, with a label-blind household hash partition fixed before any prediction existed.
\textbf{Mode A} applies everything frozen: 2018 services, channels, probes, attacker candidate sets
and 2018 validation selections are scored once on 2017. \textbf{Mode B} refits only probes, priors
and attackers on 2017 fitting pools and selects on 2017 validation pools; representations,
protection matrices and nuisances are never refitted. The two are never pooled: a weaker frozen
attack after a shift is not evidence that fresh attackers fail.

Attacks are logistic regression, two restarted MLPs at 120/360 epochs, two gradient-boosted trees
and a random-feature kernel-ridge family, plus every legal projection of H's own attacks and of
singleton candidates into the coalition view, plus, for the neural arms, their public auxiliary
heads and saved-observer catch-up trajectories. Before the final partition could be read, a lock
hashed 17{,}639 inputs---protocol, comparison families, code, all interface objects, all 2017
fitting/validation releases, every fitted object and selection---and was committed and pushed.
Uncertainty is a paired household-cluster bootstrap (2{,}000 replicates over the 10{,}701 final
households) with single-step max-$|t|$ simultaneous intervals inside each pre-declared family.

\paragraph{Study~2: development refinement.} 2018 only for its registered evidence, with a
household-cluster bootstrap over the underlying cohort. The deviation from Study~1 is deliberate
and recorded: the 2017 study had one household partition shared by all three seeds and could
cluster on it directly, whereas the 2018 pools are split per seed and overlap in people, so
clusters are drawn once per replicate from the cohort and applied to every seed. Study~2 later
re-scored the 2017 partitions as \emph{exploratory} evidence, refitting probes and attackers on
2017 fitting pools and selecting on 2017 validation pools; it deliberately never calls the sealed
loader, and it computes no simultaneous intervals there.

\paragraph{Data independence.} This is transport between public survey years of the same state and
age band, not a fresh sample from the 2018 distribution. Public identifiers \emph{cannot} establish
that 2017 respondents are different people from 2018 respondents. Verified disjointness is limited
to published household groups within 2017. Income is nominal and unadjusted, and the annual
adjustment factor differs between years, which limits cross-year interpretation of the income
column. The same caution will apply to any future 2016 evaluation, where the income estimand
differs again.

\paragraph{What was registered in each study, and what was not.} In Study~1, the protocol, the four
comparison families, every endpoint, the uncertainty procedure and all decision rules were fixed,
hashed and pushed before the final partition could be read---but \emph{no directional prediction
was registered}. The result in \S\ref{sec:coalition} is therefore a prespecified-rule confirmation
on a sealed evaluation, not a called shot. It is also not merely exploratory: fixing the contrasts,
the endpoint families, the multiplicity correction and the stopping point before the outcome is
what makes the simultaneous intervals mean what they say. In Study~2, six directional predictions
\emph{were} registered and hashed before fitting; three of the six turned out wrong or ambiguous,
and \S\ref{sec:registered2} reports them before anything else.

\paragraph{The residence task, stated precisely.} Residence labels were excluded from every
representation fit, so no arm's map saw them. That is the sense in which residence is a held-out
task. It is \emph{not} a pristine unseen-task selection: residence outcomes have been used
repeatedly across this line of work as the utility endpoint, so the choice of residence as the
capability measure is itself a development decision. And exact output preservation does not
preserve future service accuracy under shift (\S\ref{sec:parity}).

\section{Study 1: the locked 2017 evaluation}
\label{sec:results1}

\subsection{Residence capability, and its limits}

Every augmented interface clears the $0.01$-nat residence reference over H in every seed, weighting
and mode; the spectral arms carry $0.025$--$0.030$ nats against $0.017$--$0.019$ for the frozen
neural channel J. Table~\ref{tab:criteria} adds the qualification that matters: the stronger
half-headroom reference---is the channel's residence probe within half the gap between the
published service and an \emph{unrestricted} PCA32 or richer bank---passes in only 0--2 of 3 seeds
for every interface in both modes. The channel adds real capability relative to what is already
published. It does not approach what an unrestricted representation of the same covariates would
give.

On the sealed 2017 partition, C1 has a simultaneous-interval advantage over both its equal-strength

The effect is real and it is not uniform. Of the 16 sensitive cells in F1, \textbf{14 have adjusted
intervals entirely below zero, none is above zero, and two are unresolved}: C1 versus L1 on A/sex
unweighted, $[-0.00585,+0.00020]$, and C1 versus L2 on A/sex unweighted, $[-0.00568,+0.00052]$. In
both, the point estimate favours C1 and the same endpoint is strictly better under person
weighting. So the accurate statement is that coalition conditioning lowers measured recovery
against both local controls, most clearly on the coalition endpoints and local race, with local sex
unweighted undecided. Our earlier summary said ``8 of 8 sensitive endpoints better, 0 worse under
both weightings''; that count is the endpoint~$\times$~comparator grid scored as better in
\emph{at~least~one} weighting, and as written it overstates the result. It is withdrawn
(\S\ref{sec:withdrawn}, item~11). Frozen 2018 attacks reproduce the ordering (F4), so it is not an
artifact of refitting attackers on the new year.

$+0.001$. It does not, in any of the four comparisons, even under the most permissive procedure
(a pointwise one-sided bootstrap percentile with no multiplicity adjustment). This assessment is

\label{tab:equivalence}
\end{table}

The rule as implemented is $\text{seed-mean}(\text{C1}-\text{comparator})\le 0.001$ on the
residence log loss. It is one-sided---C1 may be arbitrarily better---and it is a \emph{point}
criterion: no interval enters it. The four values are $0.00029$, $0.00010$, $0.00059$, $0.00017$,
so it passes, and would pass under a two-sided reading too.

It does not follow that the residence cost is below $0.001$. Table~\ref{tab:equivalence} shows that
\textbf{zero of four comparisons support non-inferiority at that margin}, missing it by 2\% to
35\%, and zero support two-sided equivalence. ``No statistically detected cost'' and ``a cost
bounded below $0.001$'' are different statements and only the first is supported. Our earlier
phrasing ``inside the prespecified $0.001$ band'' invited exactly the interval reading the evidence
does not support, and we withdraw it.

The same distinction applies to the harm side of the rule. The \texttt{advantage} rule requires
that \emph{no} sensitive endpoint is significantly worse. That is an absence-of-evidence condition,
not a demonstration of no harm: on the two unresolved A/sex cells, up to $5\times10^{-4}$ nats of
\emph{extra} local sex recovery is not excluded. We therefore show point estimates and intervals
rather than saying an endpoint is unaffected.

\subsection{The mechanism is not competitive with the frozen neural channel}
\label{sec:notcompetitive}

C1 is significantly worse than J on \emph{all four} sensitive endpoints under \emph{both}
weightings, and worse on both source probes (income $+0.0081$, employment $+0.0135$, intervals
excluding zero). It is better only on residence, $-0.0093\,[-0.0150,-0.0036]$
(Figure~\ref{fig:forest}, right panel). The registered secondary decision F3 is therefore recorded
as not supported, and its local-race failure clause also fires.

Two things this does \emph{not} say. It does not say J dominates: J carries materially less
residence capability than every spectral arm, so neither channel Pareto-dominates the other and we
make no such claim. And it does not say the mechanism is behind the state of the art, because J is
an internal channel and no external method was run (\S\ref{sec:related}). The honest statement is
the narrow one: at matched width, under identical attacks, the spectral channel discloses
significantly more of both protected attributes to both views than this particular adversarially
trained channel, while supplying more of the authorised capability. ``Same width'' is a
\emph{capacity} control and says nothing about whether their utility is comparable. It is not,
which is why \S\ref{sec:matching} matches them on it.

\subsection{Matching the two channels on average utility}
\label{sec:matching}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/withholding.pdf}

always released; an independent visible $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ branch, fixed per person, decides

both exactly $p$ times their full-release values, so each channel traces a straight line from the

is printed as $>1$ rather than hidden.}
\label{fig:withholding}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\input{tables/withholding_matching.tex}
\caption{Post-hoc average-utility matching, Mode B, unweighted.
$p^\star=\text{gain}_{\text{C1}}/\text{gain}_{\text{source}}$ is the withholding probability at
which a comparator's \emph{mean} residence gain equals C1's; it is identified in closed form

\label{tab:matching}
\end{table}

Because the routed family mixes per-person \emph{losses}, expected residence gain over H is exactly
$p$ times the full-release gain, and expected additional recovery is exactly $p$ times the
full-release increment. The probability matching a target's \emph{mean} residence gain is therefore
identified in closed form. This yields two findings the fixed-$p$ grid did not surface.

First, \textbf{J cannot be made as useful as C1 by any withholding schedule}: it would need
$p^\star=1.49$. Nor can E ($1.07$), L025 ($1.62$) or L20 ($1.56$). The C1-versus-J comparison is not
two points on one traversable frontier. Second, \textbf{the ordering survives matching in both
directions}. Where matching is identifiable, C1 beats withheld A0 ($p^\star=0.976$) and withheld S0
($p^\star=0.929$) on all four sensitive endpoints; and C1 withheld down to J's residence level
($p^\star=0.670$) still leaks far more than J on all four ($0.0303$ against $0.0076$ on A/race).

Both statements are \textbf{post-hoc average-utility matching}: $p^\star$ is estimated from the same
data as the outcome, so the fixed-$p$ intervals do not transfer to it, and it matches an average over
people who receive different information. The original fixed-$p$ analysis is unchanged, and on it
neither family robustly dominates: withholding dominates a given spectral arm in 1--3 of 180 fixed
comparisons and a spectral arm dominates withholding in 0--3 of 180. A finite list of controls
failing to dominate C1 is not evidence that C1 is efficient.

\subsection{Support limitations}

Race class 3 (Alaska Native alone) has one person in the final partition and none in 2017 attacker
validation, so class-specific recall and AUROC are undefined for it. Full nine-class log loss is
always scored with a fixed $10^{-12}$ probability floor and renormalisation; no category is dropped.
A comparable-category diagnostic restricted to classes supported in every fitting pool of both modes
reproduces the F1 race differences almost exactly (C1 $-$ L1 on A/race: $-0.0137$ against $-0.0137$
on all rows). \textbf{A class without support is unmeasured, not protected.}

\section{Study 2: a nonlinear penalty and a rank intervention}
\label{sec:results2}

Study~2 asked whether the gap in \S\ref{sec:notcompetitive} is closed by making the penalty act on
nonlinear functions of the channel, by reducing the released rank, or by both. Its evidence is ACS
2018 development data throughout; it is not a second confirmation of anything.

\subsection{The rank question was settled without fitting anything}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\input{tables/rank_spectrum.tex}
\caption{Outcome-free rank diagnostic, computed from already-saved 2018 arm spectra before any new
fit. $r_+=\min(16,\#\{\lambda_i(U-P)>\tau\})$ under the existing relative tolerance convention. No
ACS outcome, reserved label, attack or fit is involved.}
\label{tab:rank}
\end{table}

$r_+=16$ in every seed and for every original objective, and not one of the top sixteen directions
is nonpositive. Eigenvalue-sign rank selection therefore makes \emph{no change} on this interface:
the reduced-rank recipe is an exact alias of the rank-16 recipe, and that column of the design is
void by alias. The predeclared branch fired instead, and a rank-8 sensitivity was run for both
penalty families and all three policies. \textbf{Rank 8 is a compression intervention, not
eigenvalue-sign selection}, and it is labelled that way everywhere.

The structural reading is that $U_{\mathrm{raw}}=(V^\top\!R/n)(R^\top\!V/n)$ with $R$ of width 32,
so $\operatorname{rank}(U)\le 32$; measured, $U$ has exactly 32 eigenvalues above tolerance and none
below, and since the penalties are positive semidefinite,
$\#\text{pos}(U-\lambda P)\le\#\text{pos}(U)$. We report the measured counts as the finding.
\textbf{We do not claim that $\operatorname{rank}(U)=32$ by itself implies that all sixteen selected
eigenvalues of $U-P$ are positive}: that needs an inertia argument with assumptions we did not
establish. The measurement is what supports the conclusion. And $r_+$ is the retained rank of the
\emph{old fixed linear-moment} objective; the refined objective has no fixed matrix, so neither the
rank statement nor Ky Fan optimality transfers to it.

\subsection{What was registered, and how it turned out}
\label{sec:registered2}

Six directional predictions were registered and hashed before the fit phase started.
\textbf{Three of the six were wrong or ambiguous.}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item[P1] $r_+=16$ in all three seeds, so sign-based rank selection changes nothing ---
  \textbf{confirmed} (and checkable without a fit).
\item[P2] The refinement lowers its own training objective below the original solution at rank 16
  --- \textbf{confirmed}, 18/18 conditions, gains $0.017$--$0.146$.
\item[P3] The best new candidate keeps additional A/race recovery \emph{above} J's in $\ge 2$ of 3
  seeds --- \textbf{not confirmed}: 1 of 3 seeds unweighted, 2 of 3 person-weighted; the paired
  interval on the difference contains zero.
\item[P4] Training-objective improvement will \emph{not} transfer to independent-attack improvement
  --- \textbf{refuted in its qualitative form} (\S\ref{sec:attribution}).
\item[P5] Rank-8 compression costs $>0.002$ nats of residence gain in $\ge 2$ of 3 seeds ---
  \textbf{literally met in 4 of 6 policy$\times$family cells, but the substantive claim fails}: the
  seed-mean cost is $\le 0.002$ everywhere and the sign flips across seeds.
\item[P6] No new candidate satisfies the primary coordination rule against both local controls ---
  \textbf{confirmed}: all eight registered coordination cells are NOT SUPPORTED.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The rotation diagnostic}
\label{sec:rotation}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.60\textwidth]{figures/rotation.pdf}
\caption{For each condition and seed, the share of the refinement's training-objective gain that a
\emph{separately optimised, information-preserving} orthogonal transformation of the original
solution's subspace also reaches. Bars are condition means. Mean $0.628$, range $0.367$--$0.907$
over 18 conditions. The rotation search is itself a bounded optimisation, stopping on an update
budget or a failed line search, so each value is an attained share, not a supremum --- and it is a
property of the \emph{objective}, not an accounting of what the optimiser did.}
\label{fig:rotation}
\end{figure}

The utility term, the original linear penalty and the information content of the release are all
invariant under $W\mapsto WQ$ for orthogonal $Q$. The refined penalty is not: measured, the
nonlinear penalty changes by up to $7.5\times10^{-3}$ under such a rotation while utility changes by
$\le 3.3\times10^{-16}$ and the original penalty by $\le 2.8\times10^{-17}$. So the refined
objective is genuinely coordinate-dependent, and a rotation that provably changes nothing an
attacker can recover can nonetheless improve it.

The cause is localised exactly and reproduced on a synthetic fixture: summing degree-two monomials
$a\le b$ with equal weight computes $\sum_a M_{aa}^2+\sum_{a<b}M_{ab}^2$ instead of
$\|M\|_F^2$, and only the latter survives $M\mapsto Q'MQ$. Weighting off-diagonals by $\sqrt 2$
restores exact invariance ($<10^{-12}$ against about 10\% slack). Per-feature standardisation adds
about 1\%; the Fourier block's residual slack is Monte-Carlo error that shrinks with feature count.

\textbf{What this diagnostic is, and what it is not.} It demonstrates a weakness of the objective:
a quantity that should be a function of the released information is not. It is \emph{not} a causal
budget partition of the optimiser's work. We previously wrote that roughly 63\% of the optimiser's
progress was ``provably inert'' and the remaining 37\% ``bought'' the measured privacy reductions,
and predicted that removing the inert part would let the same compute buy more. That is withdrawn
(\S\ref{sec:withdrawn}, item~13). The measured quantity is the share of the training gain
\emph{also attainable} by a separately optimised invariant transformation; the two searches are
different optimisations over different feasible sets, so their values do not decompose the actual
trajectory into an effective part and a wasted part, and nothing here predicts what a repaired
objective would achieve. The defect is a reason to fix the construction before spending a
confirmation year on it, which is what the next-step specification does; it is not a reason to
expect the fix to succeed.

\subsection{The frozen-nuisance diagnostic}

The frozen nuisances barely beat a constant prior on the protected attributes: across the local and
coalition roles and all three seeds, sex by $0.03$--$0.66\%$ of the prior loss and race by
$0.96$--$2.35\%$. The same nuisance family reaches $8.1$--$8.9\%$ on public coverage, an
\emph{authorised} attribute, so the gap is specific to the protected targets rather than a property
of the nuisance machinery. On these fitted models the protected residual is therefore close to the
\emph{marginal} residual, which is one reason an $H$-conditioned penalty had limited room to differ
from the marginal one in this study.

\textbf{This is a diagnostic of those frozen nuisance models and nothing more.} It does \emph{not}
show that $H$ carries little population information about sex or race; it does not show that the
fitted $m_j(H)$ are accurate conditional means; and it does not show that conditional and marginal
protection coincide in general. A restricted nuisance class can miss information and can miss
conditional interactions entirely, and a misspecified $m(H)$ can manufacture a penalty more than ten
times the oracle value under \emph{exact} conditional independence---which one of our own fixtures
demonstrates.

\subsection{Optimisation quality, stated within its limits}

The refinement never returned its initial point in any of the 18 conditions; both restarts
completed; and orthogonality held to $\max|W^\top W-I|\le 1.4\times10^{-15}$. Those checks rule out
three specific failures. \textbf{They do not establish that the nonconvex objective was optimised
well.} The original fixed-matrix solver has a restricted global optimum by Ky Fan; the refinement
has no such guarantee, and a suboptimal solution remains a live explanation for its results.

\subsection{Exploratory cross-year reuse, which cuts against the candidate}

Study~2 also re-scored the 2017 partitions. That evidence is \textbf{exploratory}: the 2017 seal was
already spent by Study~1, so those rows are now an exposed development pool. Probes and attackers
were fitted on 2017 fitting pools and selected on 2017 validation pools, so selection still never
saw the evaluation rows, and \emph{no simultaneous intervals are computed there by design}. Years
are reported separately and rows are never pooled.

On that partition the two 2018 attribution effects reproduce in direction at the seed mean, and
nine conditions reproduce the historical arms digit for digit, confirming the aliasing end to end.
But the comparison against J is \emph{worse} here, not better: J has lower additional recovery than
\texttt{nlr8-C1} on all four family sensitive endpoints ($0.0013/0.0039/0.0069/0.0015$ against
$0.0105/0.0155/0.0127/0.0154$), while the candidate keeps its residence advantage ($0.0267$ against
$0.0189$). The one place the 2018 result came closest to J---parity on both race endpoints---does
not carry over. Per-seed direction agreement with 2018 is only moderate (188 of 288 seed-level
comparisons). Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} shows both 2017 roles side by side. We report this because
it cuts against the candidate; omitting it would be the selective reading.

\section{Claims this revision withdraws or narrows}
\label{sec:withdrawn}

Stated explicitly, because each was in one of our own earlier documents. Items 1--10 were already
corrected in the previous revision and are repeated here so this paper is self-contained; items
11--16 are new in this integration. Almost all are wording, scope or counting: no numerical result
changed, and all 90 Study~1 endpoints reproduce under an independently written scorer to
$1.1\times10^{-15}$.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item ``\emph{Residence difference inside the prespecified $\pm0.001$ band.}'' The rule is one-sided
and applies to a point estimate. Zero of four interval comparisons support non-inferiority at that
margin. The registered rule still passed.
\item ``\emph{What changed is resolution, not direction.}'' Magnitudes changed by $0.054\times$ to
$14.8\times$; sample size alone does not explain the newly significant result.
\item ``\emph{Every F1 endpoint keeps its development sign (20/20).}'' True of seed means; 13 of 20
in every seed.
\item ``\emph{C1 versus J fails on local race.}'' It fails on all four sensitive endpoints under both
weightings, plus both source probes.
\item ``\emph{Several times more sex and race.}'' Scale-dependent: $6.0\times$ on increments,
$2.6\times$ in absolute terms. We now quote the paired difference.
\item ``\emph{Under frozen transfer every spectral arm fails in 0--1 of 3 seeds.}'' The underlying
column counts \emph{passing} seeds; the arms pass in 0--1 of 3, i.e.\ fail in 2--3 of 3.
\item ``\emph{The gap is the surrogate, not the solver.}'' Global optimality of the fixed matrix
excludes a solver failure and nothing else. Six candidate causes remain unseparated. We now say
``consistent with''.
\item ``\emph{If a nonlinear penalty also loses, the conclusion generalises to the closed-form
spectral family.}'' Withdrawn entirely. Two arms failing is evidence about two arms; and a penalty
acting on nonlinear functions of $Z$ is provably not a trace form, so such a successor is not in
that family at all.
\item ``\emph{Two lock amendments.}'' Three exist. All are code-only; the third post-dates every
final-partition read.
\item \emph{Reporting the H-relative residence gain without the half-headroom failure.} Corrected in
Table~\ref{tab:criteria}.
\item \textbf{``\emph{8 of 8 sensitive endpoints better, 0 worse, under both weightings}'' (F1).}
The denominator is 16 cells, of which 14 are strictly better, 0 worse and 2 unresolved. The
registered \texttt{advantage} rule---a different object---fired in 4 of 4 cells
(\S\ref{sec:coalition}, Table~\ref{tab:denominators}).
\item \textbf{``\emph{No endpoint significantly worse}'' for the nonlinear-penalty factor.} One is:
\texttt{nlr8-L1} against \texttt{lin8-L1} on A/race, unweighted, $+0.00862$ with an adjusted interval
excluding zero (\S\ref{sec:attribution}).
\item \textbf{``\emph{Roughly 37\% of the surrogate movement bought the measured reductions, so
removing the provably inert 63\% should let the same compute buy more.}''} Withdrawn as a causal
claim and as a prediction. The diagnostic reports an attainable share under a separately optimised
invariant transformation, not a decomposition of the optimiser's trajectory
(\S\ref{sec:rotation}).
\item \textbf{``\emph{Not an optimisation artifact.}''} The three completed checks exclude three
specific failures and do not establish good optimisation of a nonconvex objective.
\item \textbf{``\emph{The protected residual is close to the marginal residual, so any
$H$-conditioned penalty has limited room to differ, whatever function class it uses.}''} Narrowed
to a statement about the frozen nuisance models actually fitted.
\item \textbf{``\emph{2016's admission has never been run.}''} It has: 2016 is admitted on
provenance and schema with a prospective label-blind split prepared, and remains \textbf{unscored}
(Table~\ref{tab:evidence}). The reciprocal stale claim---that the other terminal was still
running---is also removed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Three seeds are three fixed fitted systems on one cohort, not population replications. The
bootstraps cover people, not retraining variability and not Census design variance; PWGTP replicate
weights were not used. Attack strength is a floor, not a guarantee. Only recipient A receives a
channel. Transport is between public survey years of the same state and age band, and cross-year
person distinctness cannot be established from public identifiers. Income is nominal and
unadjusted, and its estimand differs again in 2016, which any future evaluation there must handle.
The evaluation refits attackers but not the representation, so it does not test a mechanism
retrained on the new year. The 2018 pools have been used repeatedly and are development, not
confirmation; the 2017 seal is spent; Study~2's evaluated maps carry a known, unrepaired
coordinate-dependence defect. No external published method was executed, so no competitiveness
claim against the literature is made or supported.

\textbf{Numerical recovery.} Three code-only lock amendments are recorded. The first fixed a
release-file write race; the second fixed rare load-dependent prediction corruption found by an
independent cross-process replay; the third changed three published tables from CSV to gzip CSV and
post-dates every final read. Machine memory pressure was the \emph{observed condition} under which
the corruption appeared; it is not a demonstrated cause, and we do not claim one. The repair does
not depend on the diagnosis: every prediction is computed twice and must agree, all final units were
rescored, originals are preserved, the post-fix replay found 0 mismatches in 59{,}874 predictions
with 0 selection changes in 37{,}122 validation predictions, and Study~2's independent
cross-process replay of 27{,}540 stored predictions matched bitwise with the machine at swap
capacity throughout.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A service that has already published predictions can add a reusable channel beside them without
changing a single released number, and can steer that channel away from directions a recipient or a
colluding pair would use to infer sex and race. On a locked, previously unused survey year, the
coalition-conditioning knob does what it claims against both of its local controls---14 of 16
sensitive cells strictly better, none worse, two unresolved---under simultaneous intervals and both
weightings. It also costs residence utility by an amount the data cannot bound below the $0.001$
nats our registered rule used, and the channel it steers remains significantly worse than a frozen
adversarially trained channel of the same width on every sensitive endpoint, an ordering that
survives matching the two on average utility in both directions. A refinement designed to close
that gap moved measured recovery substantially in the intended direction, failed every one of its
eight registered coordination cells, remained significantly worse than the neural channel on local
sex, looked worse still on an exploratory cross-year look, and turned out to rest on a
coordinate-dependent objective.

The defensible contribution is the problem formulation and the evaluation design: fixed published
outputs as a hard constraint, recipient-specific and coalition-specific audit roles, fresh and
frozen attackers never pooled, absolute and incremental recovery reported side by side, negative
increments retained unclipped, denominators printed beside every count, and a sealed temporal
transport with the multiplicity fixed in advance. Both method results are negative and we report
them as such.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Deterministic seeds throughout; every unit writes a hash-bound completion record; the lock refuses
to open the final partition if any hashed input changed. All 90 pre-declared Study~1 endpoints were
regenerated from stored per-person predictions by an independently written scorer that shares no
computation function with the original report code, agreeing to $1.1\times10^{-15}$ on point
estimates and $2.4\times10^{-15}$ on interval endpoints, with identical critical values; candidate
selection was re-derived from stored validation losses with 0 mismatches in 924 cells; and the lock
was re-verified over all 17{,}639 hashed inputs with every amendment applied. Study~2's comparison
arithmetic was verified independently from its published CSVs. Every table and figure in this paper
is generated by one script from committed evidence files, each recorded with a sha256 in
\texttt{MANIFEST.json}; no number was typed by hand. Commands, file map and software versions:
\texttt{REPRODUCE\_PAPER.md}. Fitted objects, releases and per-person arrays stay local; compact
aggregates, code, tables and figures are published.
