# Prospective ACS 2016 addendum

We evaluated the two frozen, predeclared operating points Q and D17 on a previously unused ACS 2016 California cohort, with 23,684 final-pool people in 15,928 households. The panel included the historical J release, three anchor fits sharing the same households, deterministic D17/D33 controls, direct and supervised-erasure controls, and matched 75% randomization and withholding controls. The code, release laws, attacker and utility-probe recipes, fitting selections, estimands, and inference thresholds were locked before final-partition labels were read. No mechanism was fitted or selected on 2016 outcomes.

Neither release satisfied the predeclared ten-clause primary conjunction. Q's task log-loss improvements versus J were −0.003266 nats unweighted and −0.003996 under PWGTP; their one-sided 97.5% upper bounds were −0.001505 and −0.001871, above the required −0.003 margin. D17's corresponding improvements were −0.004742 and −0.005142, with upper bounds −0.002913 and −0.002951, also just above the margin. Both releases passed all eight registered sensitive-recovery clauses relative to J; each therefore passed 8/10 clauses but failed the complete claim. These are uncertainty-adjusted prospective decisions, distinct from the historical 2018 supervised development estimates.

The separate, multiplicity-corrected 70-endpoint family gives no complete advantage for Q over matched simple controls. Q improved task loss relative to RR75 and W75 under both weightings, but had significantly higher measured sensitive recovery in six of eight endpoints against each. Relative to D17 and D33, Q was worse on unweighted task loss and had no simultaneously established sensitive-recovery advantage. A claim that stochastic optimization improves the task/privacy frontier over these controls, or is a novel optimization advantage by itself, is not supported. The test does show a measured benefit against J on the registered sensitive-recovery clauses; it does not certify arbitrary attacker resistance or coalition-specific privacy for Q.

Every final score is an exact expectation over one released token, with no per-person Q row disclosed to the recipient. Paired household uncertainty uses one common 10,000-draw bootstrap over the shared household population; anchors are averaged, not treated as independent samples. All 156 fitting and 156 scoring units completed without a unit failure, and 20 primary, 70 secondary and 114 descriptive endpoints were evaluated. H service outputs were byte-identical across all 30 interfaces; separate 2016 final-pool mean service accuracies were 0.87413 (income), 0.89052 (civilian work), and 0.75992 (public coverage). Independent re-scoring reproduced all 156 stored expected losses within `3.55e-15` per person and all 204 point estimates within `1.39e-17`. Full numeric evidence, per-anchor values, service metrics, independent replay and private restoration provenance are linked in [RESEARCH_DECISION.md](RESEARCH_DECISION.md) and the adjacent result files. Failure of the full claim should not be described as equivalence or as demonstrated excess disclosure.
