# Team A prior work and claim boundary

Primary sources were checked on 2026-10-08. These are distinct settings; none grants a privacy certificate for the proposed Adult channel.

| Source | Verified setting and relevant point | Boundary for Team A |
|---|---|---|
| [Liao, Kosut, Sankar and Calmon, *Privacy Under Hard Distortion Constraints* (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00063) | Studies pointwise/per-release hard distortion with maximal α-leakage; the channel itself may randomize. | CCM's per-person, all-label guard is a different, failed Adult geometry. Team A uses observed-label **mean** NLL/Brier, so it inherits no hard guarantee. |
| [Kalantari, Kosut and Sankar, *Information-Theoretic Privacy with General Distortion Constraints* (2017; v3 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05468) | Treats random mappings under expected distortion and tail-distortion constraints, with mutual-information leakage. | Randomization under mean cost is established. The Team A finite-bank fitted objective is a modelled criterion, not population leakage. |
| [Cai, Zhang and Khalili, *Privacy-Aware Randomized Quantization via Linear Programming* (UAI 2024)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v244/cai24a.html) | Gives unbiased discrete randomized quantizers satisfying differential privacy and optimizes them with LP. | Its DP condition and unbiasedness are not Team A's attribute-recovery and confidence criteria. LP is a known tool, not a novelty claim. |
| [Taylor, Vippathalla and Coon, *Adaptive Privacy of Sequential Data Releases Under Collusion* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21859) | Online adaptive releases from a full database; previous realized releases may condition the next release. Expected distortion and mutual information characterize utility; privacy is MI about their database. | Team A's two local, non-adaptive samplers cannot read the other score or realized token. Its SEQ-12/21 controls are matched local adaptations, **not** a reproduction of Taylor's algorithm. |
| [Makhdoumi et al., *From the Information Bottleneck to the Privacy Funnel* (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.1774) | Probabilistic privacy mappings, MI objectives under log loss, greedy nonconvex privacy-funnel search. | Team A's Bayes guessing objective and measured finite-attack AUC are different. The broad privacy-utility idea is prior art. |

- `research/pcrl-adaptive-release-v1` at `2ce5d171f079f759eb755a21ab415765ea234479`, `results/pcrl_adaptive_release_v1/METHOD.md` and `RESEARCH_DECISION.md`: this **did fit** a supervised ACS 32-state-to-17-token stochastic Q, with fixed-decoder expected-token task coefficients, retained frozen-attack cuts, a same-bank deterministic control and one sampled public token. It appended the token beside unchanged A-side service features and left B's historical service unchanged. Its locked 2018 development assessment established no competitive route; a fixed-bank LP/MILP gap did not become a measured privacy/utility advantage. Team A's mathematical row-stochastic channel is the **same generic mechanism class**. What changes is the empirical setting and release contract: two Adult frozen **task-score** inputs, sampled replacement probability-bank outputs for both recipients, original mean NLL/Brier caps against U0/Ucal, and aligned SEX recovery for both recipients and their coalition. These differences motivate a transfer benchmark, not algorithmic novelty.
- `research/pcrl-stochastic-channel-v1`, `results/pcrl_stochastic_channel_v1/REGISTRATION.md` and `RESEARCH_DECISION.md`: the ACS role-constrained Q used a finite **A-side PCA code** and would **append** a sampled Z to unchanged `[H_A,Z_J]`, with B unchanged. It considered conditional-information constraints for residence/other roles. Its capacity gate failed before Q was fitted on ACS. Its synthetic fixtures established only finite-law possibility. Team A instead **replaces** each Adult task-score interface with a sampled probability-bank output for income and occupation, optimizes observed-label mean NLL/Brier against U0/Ucal and audits SEX local and coalition recovery. It does not claim a new general Q algorithm merely because the application differs.
- `research/pcrl-stochastic-replacement-overnight-v1`, `results/pcrl_stochastic_replacement_overnight_v1/RESEARCH_DECISION.md`: the later ACS contract removed unchanged `Z_J`, but its registered score/code families failed a utility screen before any ACS Q was fitted. That result does not prove all replacement channels fail.
- `research/pcrl-decision-preserving-compression-v1` and `research/pcrl-confidence-capacity-v1`: deterministic Adult class-preserving score cells, task-only/local/sequential/joint controls, and the asymmetric i8o64 capacity. - `research/pcrl-adult-learned-decoder-release-v1` and `research/pcrl-heldout-calibration-v1`: fixed tokens with changed probability decoders. - `research/pcrl-confidence-constrained-mechanism-v1`, `results/pcrl_confidence_constrained_mechanism_v1/PROTOCOL.md` §§3–6: a hard pointwise/all-label guard, deterministic covers and a finite-law stochastic-local LP. The Adult guard required many singleton tokens and extensive raw fallback; no Adult privacy Q was fitted. Team A retains the original mean allowances, permits randomized outputs whose expected losses meet those allowances, and permits no raw fallback. Its finite-law objective remains familiar Bayes guessing accuracy.

## Novelty position

This lane tests whether an established random-channel tool improves an Adult **score-only** release under a matched confidence and coalition contract. A win could be an implementation or empirical operating-point improvement. It would not itself establish a new solver, privacy theorem, or feature-sharing method. Randomization earns component attribution only if the one-hot matched ablation loses; joint design earns attribution only beyond both sequential orders and every stronger non-joint control.
