# Proposed next finite experiment: source-guarded protection updates

**Proposal only; not launched.** Test whether preventing the entire protection displacement from opposing each native source task locally improves the independent source-utility floor enough to make coalition/local comparisons informative. The present study's main obstacle is not an untested coefficient: all 18 fixed systems fail the seed-1 coverage floor, and F has no nonidentical close source-plus-residence overlap at δ=.001. This experiment changes the update rule while retaining the original source metric and strong local controls.

The hypothesis is falsifiable: guarding the protection increment may recover source feasibility and create comparable useful points, while J may or may not retain a SEX advantage over the two fixed strong local controls. It may also fail because the source-training recipe, probe generalization or representation capacity is the limiting factor. Native fitting gradients are an intervention target, not the success metric.

## Precisely defined Adam displacement guard

Let θ and the complete Adam state be the actual state immediately before one authorized training update, after the scheduled detached observer updates. Evaluate the ordinary weighted source objective and the full condition objective on the same minibatch and current tensors. The J/Iplus coefficients and normalization stay exactly as in this study. The guard covers the entire protection increment: both the ordinary −.1 individual term and the additional −β local/coalition term, not only the β term. Source heads retain their original task support and masks; no reserved outcome enters any gradient.

1. Clone the current parameter tensors **and** Adam first/second moments and step counters into two disposable proposals. From these identical states, compute one source-only Adam proposal θ_source and one full-objective Adam proposal θ_full. These are calculations of candidate displacements, not two actual training steps. No optimizer, RNG or batch schedule is advanced twice.
2. Define Δ_source = θ_source−θ and r = θ_full−θ_source. Restrict r to the original protection-gradient support: for F, only the two mapper parameter blocks; for P, the mappers and their source-head blocks, as in the existing public-probability path. All other displacement coordinates follow the source-only proposal. F source heads consequently retain their ordinary source-objective update.
3. Compute the three separate native, unweighted masked source-loss gradients g_income, g_employment and g_coverage at θ on this minibatch, restricted to that same support. A task with no eligible minibatch rows has a zero constraint and is logged; neither rows nor masks are changed.
4. Find the unique nearest displacement v to r satisfying g_t·v≤0 for each of the three tasks. Use Euclidean projection in parameter coordinates, in float64. Implement a deterministic convex three-halfspace projection by enumerating the at most eight active subsets, using a fixed SVD relative rank tolerance 1e−12, requiring nonnegative multipliers and checking all inequalities with roundoff tolerance 1e−12×max(1,‖g_t‖‖v‖). Zero is always feasible; nonfinite inputs stop and preserve the affected unit for diagnosis rather than silently skipping the guard. A fixed lexicographic active-set ordering resolves numerical ties. These are numerical cases, not a model-selection search.
5. Apply **one actual parameter update**, θ_next = θ + Δ_source + v. Retain the full-objective proposal's Adam first/second moments and its once-incremented step counters. Discard the source-only proposal's optimizer state. This explicit choice keeps the full-gradient moment history while projecting its current parameter displacement; do not describe it as vanilla Adam on a different loss.

No extra optimizer fitting steps are taken. Record the raw/full/source displacements, projection distance, active constraints, before/after dot products, actual parameter supports and the retained Adam-state hashes at predeclared diagnostic points. The source-only control has r=0 and must reproduce the ordinary source-only update exactly. Detached observers still receive their ordinary steps and are not projected.

This condition is **linearized at the current minibatch and parameter state**. It says the incremental displacement attributed to protection has nonpositive first-order native source-loss change relative to the source-only proposal. It does not guarantee finite-step native loss, full-fitting loss, generalization, independent probe utility, or the original PCA32 +.01 allowance. The retained full-gradient moments may affect later steps, and the guard may weaken protection. These are measured outcomes, not reasons to change the rule after observing results. The projection is a standard optimization building block, not a novelty or privacy claim.
