# Closest methods and the scope of a possible result

This is a 2018 development study on repeatedly used data. The proposed task-aligned constraint exchange is an **adaptation**, not a novelty claim. The table was written from the linked primary texts and pinned local method records before outer assessment. A fixed-bank optimization certificate and a measured advantage are separate propositions.

| Closest method | Mathematical overlap | Change in this implementation | Necessary assumption or limitation | Matched baseline | What an empirical win would establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Agarwal et al., 2018, *A Reductions Approach to Fair Classification*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/agarwal18a/agarwal18a.pdf) | Constrained empirical objective, randomized decisions and cost-sensitive/oracle updates. Their fair-classification constraints are linear conditional moments, and their finite-sample result assumes a controlled classifier family and concentration error. | Our variable is a 32×17 token kernel; fixed task/attack predictors make every empirical loss affine in that kernel. Constraints are fitted sensitive prediction-loss floors, including continuous H service views. | Neither their fairness guarantee nor oracle result transfers to adaptively fitted full-view attacks on repeatedly used ACS data. | Same coefficients, bank and budget for LP, MILP and bounded deterministic search. | Better *measured* frontier than matched deterministic controls; no first-use claim for randomization or constraint generation. |
| [Calmon and Fawaz, *Privacy Against Statistical Inference*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.2123); [Calmon et al., *Optimized Pre-Processing for Discrimination Prevention*](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/6988-optimized-pre-processing-for-discrimination-prevention.pdf) | Finite stochastic preprocessing channels and convex expected-distortion/privacy constraints were established before PCRL. | Our fitted target is a supervised residence task and lower bounds on declared attackers' log losses with H_A or H_A+H_B visible. | Empirical attack-bank floors are not true conditional mutual information, differential privacy, or protection against all attacks. | Historical Q's fixed cost/coarse CMI, matched D17/D_U1, and feasible deterministic release under the same bank. | Whether this particular task/attack alignment improves a finite empirical frontier, not a new finite-channel theorem. |
| [Tripathy, Wang and Ishwar, *Privacy-Preserving Adversarial Networks*](https://merl.com/publications/docs/TR2019-113.pdf) | Randomized mechanisms and adversarial sensitive prediction; its finite-alphabet case explicitly notes direct token expectation instead of sampling. | A small finite kernel is solved against retained affine cuts, while best-response fitting remains heuristic and separate from the LP. The recipient task cost is tied to a common H_A+token decoder. | Their minimax/information-theoretic interpretation depends on an expressive, effectively optimized adversary and distribution assumptions; a fixed finite bank cannot inherit it. | Matched gradient adversarial finite-channel adaptation with equal code, action set, supervised access, families and oracle budget. | An advantage over a fair gradient adaptation, if independently observed; not invention of adversarial random release. |
| [Taylor, Vippathalla and Coon, 2026, *Adaptive Privacy of Sequential Data Releases Under Collusion*](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21859v2) | Separate constraints for individual and colluding recipients, with previously released information accounted for. Their sequential problem uses mutual information and a Blahut–Arimoto-style algorithm under a specified joint law. | Here H_A and H_B are immutable existing service outputs, only A gets one added token, and the coalition is audited through its actual continuous H_A,H_B view. P1 constrains four role/attribute attack losses under both weightings. | This is one new release, not a sequential composition theorem; no all-law or repeated-draw privacy guarantee follows. | Matched local-only P1 policy with equal attack and fit work, plus stronger local comparator. | Coalition-specific benefit only if AB improves without local harm against that control. |
| [Belrose et al., *LEACE*](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/d066d21c619d0a78c5b557fa3291a8f4-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Holstege, Ravfogel and Wouters, *SPLINCE*](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/26dbc8565974cffe8a44731aa09b2aa8-Paper-Conference.pdf); archived OptNet-ARL adaptation | Linear erasure and adversarial representation optimization are strong existing alternatives on fixed H services. | Our token interface is finite and task-supervised; these restored continuous releases differ in width, input and training supervision. | LEACE/SPLINCE guarantee only declared linear moments; historical OptNet encoder weights were not persisted, though executed release arrays were. Direct paired scientific comparison requires same new audit slate and disclosure of access differences. | Reuse hash-pinned executed releases and refit a common compatible audit slate; report unmatched access explicitly. | A contextual frontier comparison, not a same-contract algorithmic advantage if access differs. |

The earlier direct-adversarial study at `106de9af…` and competitive-method study at `7f961d5c…` found no overall competitive frontier. A positive result here would have to survive fresh task probes, fresh attacks, the matched gradient adaptation, and feasible deterministic optimization; a favorable fixed-bank LP objective alone would not contradict those negative results. The later claims review at `dee54231…` also corrects a common error: a distribution-weighted information-radius calculation is not channel capacity or an all-distribution full-H certificate. No such guarantee is claimed by P1.
