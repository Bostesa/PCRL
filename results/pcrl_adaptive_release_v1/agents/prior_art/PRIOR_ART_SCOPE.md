# Prior-art scope for the adaptive-release development study

Read from the linked primary full texts on 2026-09-24 UTC. This note maps mathematical overlap; it does not certify an empirical PCRL advantage or priority over all related work.

| Primary source and precise pointer | Established result and overlap | Scope of the current study |
|---|---|---|
| [Rassouli and Gündüz, *On Perfect Privacy*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.08500), §II, Definition 1 and Proposition 1, Eq. (1); §V, Theorem 4 | With finite sensitive `X`, useful `Y`, and encoder observation `W`, an exactly private informative release exists precisely when a direction in `Null(P_X|W)` is outside `Null(P_Y|W)`. Their full-observation result distinguishes encoder access from output perturbation. Thus extra observable distinctions can change the feasible privacy/utility set even if a task statistic looks sufficient. | PCRL's B1/B2 fixtures illustrate an access restriction and context-dependent release; neither the nullspace insight nor perfect-privacy feasibility is new. ACS uses predicted risks and empirical fitted-loss constraints, not the exact joint law or unconditional `I(X;Z)=0` of this proposition. No perfect-privacy claim follows. |
| [Tripathy, Wang and Ishwar, *Privacy-Preserving Adversarial Networks*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.07008), §II-A Eqs. (1)–(3), §II-B Eqs. (4)–(5), §II-C1; Appendix C; [Allerton version](https://merl.com/publications/docs/TR2019-113.pdf), §II | PPAN already trains stochastic data-release mechanisms against an adversary, including finite-alphabet mechanisms. Its adversarial log-loss is a variational route to mutual information under its distribution/model assumptions; alternating mechanism and attack updates are established. Appendix C explicitly discusses attacker side information under stated conditions. | PCRL's finite 17-token exact expectation, relative fitted-risk floors, and stored-cut LP are an implementation and different empirical target, not invention of stochastic adversarial release or generic side-information privacy. A fitted attack bank and heuristic best responses are not PPAN's information-theoretic optimum or an arbitrary-attacker guarantee. |
| [Agarwal et al., *A Reductions Approach to Fair Classification*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/agarwal18a/agarwal18a.pdf), §2.1 Eqs. (1)–(4), §3.1 Eq. (5), §3.2 Eq. (6) and Algorithm 1, Theorem 1 | Linear constraints on conditional moments lead to Lagrangian cost-sensitive best responses, dual updates, and randomized constrained classifiers. Mixtures can improve a constrained frontier over a deterministic rule. | PCRL releases a token for later prediction; it does not establish their demographic-parity/equalized-odds guarantees. Fixed-price split scores and randomized constrained optimization are not, by themselves, novel principles. PCRL must demonstrate an advantage beyond matched deterministic and adversarial controls before attributing one to a component. |
| [Ding and Sadeghi, *A Submodularity-based Agglomerative Clustering Algorithm for the Privacy Funnel*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.06629), §II-A Eq. (1), §III-A Eqs. (2)–(5), Algorithm 1 and Theorem 1 | Privacy-guided deterministic quantization is established. Their hard clustering iteratively **merges** input symbols and searches merges for the `I(S;Xhat) - λ I(X;Xhat)` privacy-funnel objective using a difference-of-submodular-functions formulation. | PCRL's nested **splits**, supervised task log loss, public service-conditioned fitted attacks, and fixed 17-token wire differ in objective and construction. A split-score improvement is only fixed-price freedom, not a new privacy-funnel theorem or independently measured tradeoff gain. Do not claim first privacy-aware partition or quantizer. |
| [Taylor, Vippathalla and Coon, *Adaptive Privacy of Sequential Data Releases Under Collusion*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21859), §II Eq. (1), §III Eq. (3), §IV-D–E Algorithms 1–2, §VI Algorithm 3 | Previous outputs and multiple potentially colluding recipients already appear explicitly in a release problem. The paper constrains both individual `I(Rhat_k;X)` and cumulative `I(Rhat_k,Rhat^(k-1);X)`; its adaptive Blahut–Arimoto procedure has an expected-distortion optimality result under its model, while the mutual-information utility problem is nonconvex. | PCRL audits one new token with fixed A and AB service views using empirical predictor losses. It does not solve sequential composition, arbitrary collusion, or their MI-constrained problem. Do not claim first coalition-aware release or that preserving current H outputs erases already available information. |

The narrow hypothesis under test is whether **deployable task- and privacy-relevant internal distinctions**, together with a reference-feasible fixed-predictor channel update, improve independently audited 2018 development task and sensitive-recovery measurements over strong matched controls. A fixed-bank LP/MILP certificate concerns only its frozen coefficients, decoder, rows, and attack bank. The alternating decoder/attacker/partition procedure is not a globally solved convex programme. An observed advantage would be development evidence about this construction and supervision/access contract; a novel algorithmic component requires its matched ablation and further prior-art comparison. No result here turns used 2018 data into fresh confirmation, gives a population mutual-information bound, protects the joint sensitive label, or establishes repeated-release privacy.
