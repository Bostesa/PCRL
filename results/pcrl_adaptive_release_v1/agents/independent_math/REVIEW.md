## Reference feasibility

For each role and weighting, the new reference is `rho = min_a sum(A_a * Q_ref)` on the **same coefficient rows**, and each retained attack has floor `rho - delta`. Thus every attack's risk at `Q_ref` is at least its floor for `delta >= 0`. Recomputing `rho` on every bank expansion preserves the constructive D17 witness, including when a new H-only attack becomes the best reference. This proves fixed-bank feasibility, not protection against arbitrary attacks. The `reference.calibrate_reference` implementation also checks coefficient-pool identity, class order, and U/PWGTP normalization in production metadata.

The independent ranking-reversal fixture has a selected attack at loss 0.5 versus an H-only attack at 0.6 on the *selection* pool. On coefficient rows, their D17 risks are 0.7 and 0.4. A historical floor of 0.7 imposed on the H-only cut is impossible for every channel because its loss is always 0.4. The new min-bank floor is 0.4 and D17 is feasible. The previously stored hash-pinned closed-coefficient aggregate replay reports 312 cuts per anchor; for anchors 1 and 2 the old maximum D17 violations were 0.0095631768 and 0.0074400880 nats, including invariant H-only unavoidable gaps 0.0080164577 and 0.0056375454. The calibrated D17 violation was zero in that replay. These figures are historical diagnostic evidence, not new candidate outcomes or a retrospective change to the closed study.

## Fixed-bank optimization

For attack constraints `A_j · Q >= floor_j`, the minimization Lagrangian is `C · Q + sum_j lambda_j (floor_j - A_j · Q)` with `lambda_j >= 0`. The priced row is therefore `C - sum_j lambda_j A_j`. The lower bound is `sum_t min_z priced[t,z] + sum_j lambda_j floor_j`. `fit_b.fixed_bank_dual_prices` extracts nonnegative multipliers from HiGHS' negative inequality marginals and recomputes this bound. A one-state 17-token fixture independently obtains LP objective and dual bound 0.5, versus deterministic optimum and MILP lower bound 1.0 on the **same cut**; exhaustive enumeration of all 17 deterministic maps confirms the latter. This is a strict finite-bank integrality gap only for that fixture. No adaptive or population guarantee follows.

The priced split score is nonnegative for an exact child partition at fixed prices because separate child action minima cannot exceed the common-parent minimum. An independent two-row fixture verifies `task - lambda * attack`, its sign, and a gain of 1. The score can change when multipliers, decoder, bank, or coefficient rows change; it is not a feasible-primal or held-out-gain certificate.

## Exact information fixtures

B1 enumerates all 15 partitions of four states with rational probabilities. The proposed stochastic release has `P(Z=1|S=0)=P(Z=1|S=1)=1/5`, `P(Y=1|Z=1)=9/10`, and `P(Y=1|Z=0)=2/5`; its task information is 0.0897212523 nats. The two exactly private deterministic partitions have zero task information. B2 has zero conditional sensitive information and conditional task information `(log 2)/2` for a contextual *deterministic* release. At `H=1`, where `S=T`, privacy forces the two context-blind token laws to coincide, leaving no task information at `H=0`. These are access-restriction counterexamples, not ACS efficacy predictions or novelty theorems.

## Frozen controls and claim boundaries

The A loader verifies COMPLETE inventory, final-bank selection, model and coefficient hashes, and selected-Q replay. The B loader verifies the analogous inventory, partition and channel hashes, final bank, and selected-Q feasibility on that bank. The controls use one frozen U/W balanced cost and exactly its cuts for deterministic MILP, deterministic search, gradient updates, and simple release maps. The registered constant replacement is token 0; an initial cost-optimal-token mismatch was corrected before the controls source freeze, and the focused test now checks a fixture whose cheapest token is 1. D_task is only the rowwise deterministic minimum for this **frozen decoder cost**. It is not the independently audited task optimum, a privacy-feasible control, or the historical D17 map in general.

The gradient grid uses exact finite-bank coefficients and 17-token logits, but currently performs fixed-bank channel updates. A competitive claim against a *matched adaptive adversarial algorithm* still requires equal-budget fresh attack responses, decoder opportunities, inner selection, and common final audit. The control receipt does not by itself prove a same-bank LP–MILP gap: the selected A/B channel may come from an earlier adaptation round, and the runner does not yet pin that selected channel's final-cost objective in its receipt. A gap claim requires a feasible LP upper objective from a solve on the exact frozen final cost/cuts and a valid MILP lower bound on those identical inputs, with both artifacts and residuals pinned. An MILP timeout remains unresolved, not evidence that no deterministic mechanism exists.

The fixed-bank attack constraints are empirical fitted-risk floors. Their satisfaction is separate from fresh-attack performance, component attribution, and any population privacy guarantee. Household-paired final inference and the declared selection family remain outside this review.
