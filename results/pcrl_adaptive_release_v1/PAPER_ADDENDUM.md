# Manuscript addendum: completed adaptive development study

The newly fitted PCRL adaptive release was evaluated on previously used 2018 development data with three shared-household anchors. Its locked utility-route channel did not improve residence prediction over matched D17: candidate-minus-D17 cross-entropy was +0.001450 nats (U) and +0.000914 (PWGTP), with simultaneous 95% intervals crossing the registered -0.003 improvement target. The privacy-first channel showed lower AB/SEX recovery at the point estimate (-0.003672 U; -0.003325 PWGTP relative to D17), but the simultaneous intervals did not establish the -0.002 target and residence loss worsened by +0.009539/+0.009835 nats, exceeding the +0.001 guard. Neither route passed its full registered conjunction. Do not describe these point estimates as a competitive algorithm result.

The same-row min-bank construction resolved the predecessor's infeasible reference cuts and yielded a deployable, hash-pinned 17-token release with byte-preserved service vectors. Its fixed-bank solver certificates are numerical properties of a frozen empirical programme. They do not establish strong-attacker, full-population, or repeated-release privacy. The proposed privacy-relevant child-state refinement was support-limited to the original 32 states on every anchor, so the ACS study offers no positive or negative test of that distinguishing component. Historical 2016 prospective failures of Q and D17 remain the registered prospective verdict; this 2018 work must be presented separately as adaptive development.
