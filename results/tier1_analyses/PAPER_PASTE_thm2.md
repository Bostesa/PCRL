## Paper paste — §3.1 label-shift validation

Theorem 2 binds whenever some pair of attribute values $(a, a')$ satisfies $\min\{\Pr(A=a), \Pr(A=a')\}\,d_{TV}(\pi_i^a, \pi_i^{a'}) \geq \gamma$ for the conditional task-label distributions $\pi_i^a = \Pr(Y_i \mid A = a)$. We computed this quantity directly on the full tabular populations and report the cell-level $\gamma_{\min}$ as the maximum LHS across pairs. Across all 20 (dataset, purpose, disallowed-attribute) cells none reach $\gamma_{\min} \geq 0.1$, 3 reach $\gamma_{\min} \geq 0.05$, and 12 reach $\gamma_{\min} \geq 0.01$, with the raw values ranging from 0.0009 to 0.0804. On Adult the cell-level $\gamma_{\min}$ values span $[0.011, 0.080]$, with the largest separation appearing for education assessment against income (0.080). On HMDA the cell-level $\gamma_{\min}$ values span $[0.005, 0.048]$, with the largest separation appearing for pricing analysis against race (0.048). On Diabetes the cell-level $\gamma_{\min}$ values span $[0.001, 0.039]$, with the largest separation appearing for billing audit against gender (0.039). The implication is that on these benchmarks Theorem 2 is essentially vacuous: the marginal label distribution barely shifts across attribute groups, so the bound permits arbitrarily tight invariance without predicting any task-accuracy floor. The cross-purpose leakage that the auditors in \S5 detect is therefore not the label-shift channel that Theorem 2 covers; it is the high-cardinality residual structure in $X$ that survives projection onto a low-rank LoRA adapter, which a future tighter bound would need to address.
