## Paper paste — §5.3 $\tau$ sensitivity

We swept the compliance threshold $\tau \in \{0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2\}$ on the same 60 cells (3 datasets $\times$ 3 seeds $\times$ 6--8 purpose-attribute pairs) without retraining; task accuracy is unchanged by $\tau$ since the trained encoder is fixed and only the compliance verdict moves. At the paper's headline $\tau\!=\!0.05$ the strict $R^2_{\mathrm{onehot}}\!<\!\tau$ test is satisfied by 56/60 cells and the dominant-axis $R^2_{\mathrm{DA}}\!<\!\tau$ test by 55/60. Tightening to $\tau\!=\!0.025$ keeps 52/60 strict and 49/60 DA cells compliant; tightening further to $\tau\!=\!0.01$ drops strict pass to 37/60 and DA pass to 31/60. Loosening to $\tau\!=\!0.1$ and $\tau\!=\!0.2$ raises strict pass to 59/60 and 60/60 respectively. The mean residual $R^2_{\mathrm{onehot}}$ across all 60 cells is 0.0136 (mean $R^2_{\mathrm{DA}}$ is 0.0217), so the cells that fail $\tau\!=\!0.05$ do so by a small margin: a single-percentage-point tightening of $\tau$ costs only a handful of pass verdicts, which is the qualitative claim that the headline number is robust to threshold choice.
