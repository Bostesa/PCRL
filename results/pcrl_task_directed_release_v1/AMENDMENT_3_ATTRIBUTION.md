# Prospective amendment 3: attribution and matched local frontiers

Recorded 2026-09-21 23:37:23 UTC, before comparative study outcomes have been inspected. The complete-unit benchmark remains an integrity/resource exercise. This amendment fixes the interpretation and implementation of already requested attribution comparisons; it does not change the main historical-J or competitive selection criteria, audit budgets, empirical margins, or reporting weightings.

## A matched local frontier for coalition claims

For each frozen route nominee with coalition policy C, retain its same-budget L comparison as a mechanistic diagnostic. A coalition tradeoff claim additionally compares against the best validation-eligible local-policy setting across all three registered positive budgets, with the same input code, action dictionary size and conditioning family. The three-anchor mean and one configuration shared across both weightings remain mandatory.

Utility-first local eligibility requires all eight J-relative sensitive increments at most .001. Its nominee minimizes balanced residence loss, then configuration ID. Protection-first local eligibility requires both J-relative residence-loss differences at most .001. Its nominee minimizes the maximum of the eight sensitive increments, then balanced residence loss, then configuration ID. Retain the complete attempted frontier, missing or unfinished settings, eligibility and nominee. An incomplete three-budget frontier or empty eligibility set blocks a broad coalition-frontier claim; the same-budget diagnostic remains available.

Primary and optional fine-C conditioning families must not be mixed. A fine-C coalition channel compares against fine-C local channels. Matched risk/task comparisons also require the same conditioning family; missing matched counterparts block that attribution.

## Explicit adjusted attribution conjunctions

Write utility differences as `L_candidate - L_comparator` and sensitive increments as `L_comparator - L_candidate`, using the same frozen predictors and person rows. All inequalities below refer to the corresponding Bonferroni-adjusted upper bounds from the single explicit endpoint family.

- Utility-first attribution requires strict utility improvement `< 0` under both weightings and sensitive non-inferiority `<= .001` at all eight primary sensitive endpoints.
- Protection-first attribution requires utility non-inferiority `<= .001` under both weightings, sensitive non-inferiority `<= .001` at all eight endpoints, and at least one strict sensitive improvement `< 0`.
- For a coalition-specific protection-first claim, the strict improvement must occur in at least one of the four AB endpoints: SEX or RAC1P under either weighting. A-only improvement is insufficient.
- Risk-refinement attribution requires the complete conjunction separately against both matched T0 and matched Ttask; passing against either one alone is insufficient.

The common operating constraints are also checked on evaluation: utility-first attribution requires the candidate and each comparator to satisfy the eight J-relative privacy caps; protection-first attribution requires the candidate and each comparator to satisfy both J-relative utility allowances. These cap checks, every direct comparison, same-budget diagnostic and frontier comparison are frozen in `CONTRASTS.json` and counted in `M = len(endpoints)` before evaluation.

Narrow attribution is distinct from passing the main historical-J or competitive criterion. It is not gated by the nominee's main J-relative validation screen. The nominee remains frozen from the unchanged validation-only route selection, including a descriptive nominee if necessary; no evaluation result can change its identity. Availability flags alone never establish attribution. An explicit adjusted conjunction must pass, otherwise the effect remains unresolved or descriptive.

## Registered expanded controls

When prospective branch-A metadata is supplied, merge its U33 channels into the deterministic-action family, its withholding33 settings into the withholding family, its randomized-response33 settings into the randomized-response family, and its constant/independent-token33 settings into the constant/null family. Direct codes, continuous predictors, H and supervised erasers are reused unchanged. Require the explicit extension/control registration provenance. Preserve attempted and unavailable settings, and nominate one setting from each enlarged family with the existing shared-anchor/shared-weighting rules.

No ACS outcome was read to design or test these changes. Verification uses synthetic summaries and household arrays only.
